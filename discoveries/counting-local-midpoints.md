# Counting possible midpoints without listing the interval

Given only the product 34,387, the two primes have an integer midpoint between 186 and 196 if the larger prime is at most twice the smaller. Testing what is possible at every power of 2, 3 and 5 leaves just 194. Its square exceeds the product by $57^2$, so the primes are $194-57=137$ and $194+57=251$. We located the midpoint using four interval counts and tested one integer square. [Lean certificate](../PrimeFactors/LocalMidpointLanes.lean) · [Reproducible examples](../benchmarks/local-midpoints.json)

We can count the survivors in **any subinterval** in time polynomial in the bit length of the product for a **fixed set of auxiliary primes**. An auxiliary prime can be tested to unlimited precision. On the project's 2,048-bit comparison number, using 2, 11 and 13 together ruled out 99.126% of the full balanced interval, but the exact number left still had 1,013 bits. Independent square tests on the survivors would remain impractical. The known Dirichlet-GRH isolation result uses a list of primes that grows with the input size. The bound here grows exponentially in the number of primes used. [Earlier isolation question](factor-sum-residues.md) · [Implemented count](../experiments/local_midpoint_lanes.py)

For every 2,048-bit product of two 1,024-bit primes, more than $2^{874}$ wrong midpoints in the balanced interval pass every power of every prime through 733. This gives a uniform limit on what can be inferred using just those primes. [Kernel-checked lower bound](persistent-local-survivors.md)

## Which midpoints pass at one prime?

Let $N=pq$ with distinct odd primes $p<q\le2p$ and set $h=(p+q)/2$. Its interval is $\lceil\sqrt N\rceil\le h\le\lfloor\sqrt{9N/8}\rfloor$. Over the integers, $h^2-N=((q-p)/2)^2$. Over **any** residue ring, two factors can have product $N$ and sum $2h$ exactly when $h^2-N$ is a square. Indeed, one factor $x$ gives the square $(x-h)^2$; a root $d$ gives the pair $h-d,h+d$. This proof does not divide by two in the residue ring. Let $D=h^2-N$. The previously published modular-hyperbola counts describe the number of possible sums over a full period. Here we need a representation that also counts arbitrary pieces of that period. [Lean midpoint equivalence](../PrimeFactors/LocalMidpointLanes.lean) · [Bower, Evans, Luo and Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.2930) · [Earlier prime-power counts](prime-power-counts.md)

Choose an auxiliary prime $l$ coprime to $N$ and an exponent $e\ge1$. One possible case is $h^2\equiv N\pmod {l^e}$, where $D$ vanishes. Otherwise the valuation of a square is some even $v<e$. The following short congruences on $h$ describe all the remaining cases.

- At $l=2$, put $d=\min(3,e-v)$. The condition is $h^2\equiv N+2^v\pmod {2^{v+d}}$. An odd number is a square modulo $2^r$ exactly when it is 1 modulo $2^{\min(3,r)}$. The Lean proof constructs every higher lift and proves the equivalence including the zero case. [Exact dyadic classification](../PrimeFactors/LocalMidpointLanes.lean)
- At an odd $l$, take each nonzero square $u\bmod l$. The condition is $h^2\equiv N+l^v u\pmod {l^{v+1}}$. A nonzero square modulo $l$ lifts as a square unit to every power, since its root has invertible derivative. The valuation and first nonzero digit distinguish the cases. [Unit-square lifting in Lean](../PrimeFactors/PrimePower.lean)

Each resulting root is a single arithmetic progression of midpoints. These progressions are disjoint. At two, each target with $v>0$ is odd and has at most four roots; the initial $v=0$ test works modulo at most eight and also has at most four. The terminal odd target has at most four roots. At an odd prime each target with $v>0$ is a unit with at most two roots; the initial targets modulo $l$ need at most two each. Thus the lists use at most $4(\lceil e/2\rceil+1)$ entries at two, and $(l-1)\lceil e/2\rceil+2$ at odd $l$. [Bound on dyadic roots](../PrimeFactors/DyadicMass.lean) · [Bound on odd-prime roots and lifting](smooth-factor-gaps.md)

To construct them, follow the roots of $N$ itself up its prime powers. At each even level $v$, the target $N+l^v u$ shares the first $v$ digits of $N$. Branch from the same live roots, checking at most three more digits at two or one elsewhere. If the roots of $N$ disappear, all deeper branches are empty. At fixed $l$ this takes $O_l(e)$ lifting steps on integers of $O(e\log l)$ bits. The implementation explicitly checks digits, so the constant depends on $l$. [Constructor and interval counter](../experiments/local_midpoint_lanes.py)

All $l$ children of a progression together give exactly their parent. After constructing the lists, combine complete sets of children, starting at the highest power and working down. The remaining progressions are the largest ones contained in the accepted set. Indeed, two overlapping progressions modulo powers of the same prime are nested; a larger contained progression would have had all its children available to merge. Every exact cover by progressions of prime-power stride needs at least one per maximal class. The merged list attains that minimum. [Lean equality for a full set of children](../PrimeFactors/LocalMidpointLanes.lean)

## From finite exponents to every power

Within the balanced interval $0\le D\le D_{\max}=h_{\max}^2-N$. A zero discriminant passes every power. For odd $l$, choose $l^e>D_{\max}$; any positive $D$ has its first nonzero base-$l$ digit before position $e$. For two, choose $e=\max(1,\operatorname{bitlength}(D_{\max})+2)$, which also exposes the next two bits. The decision for every midpoint in the interval is then final at that prime. The same lists answer every subinterval query.

For distinct primes, choose one progression from each list and combine by the Chinese remainder theorem. For a resulting residue $r\bmod M$, its first representative at or above $L$ is $f=L+((r-L)\bmod M)$. It contributes zero if $f>U$, otherwise $1+\lfloor(U-f)/M\rfloor$. Summing counts each surviving midpoint once. If a partial CRT class contains at most one midpoint, the code tests it directly in the remaining lists. The elementary worst-case enumeration visits $O_P((1+\log N)^{|P|})$ combinations for fixed prime set $P$, with polynomial bit cost per combination. It can be expensive to add more primes. If a query returns one survivor, binary subdivision locates it, and a final integer square and product check tests whether it yields the factors. [Independent exhaustive tests against modular factor pairs](../tests/test_local_midpoint_lanes.py)

The three exponents on the 2,048-bit example are 2,047, 591 and 553. Before merging, their lists contain 4,092, 5 and 6 progressions respectively. Merging cuts the first list to 2,044 and preserves the exact count. Three paired runs alternating order on an arm64 Mac took medians of 1.015 seconds before merging and 0.589 seconds after merging. These are times for the full interval count. Here 11 and 13 are primes at which the product is a nonsquare; including all higher powers of either adds no further restrictions beyond the first. A single full-interval count sufficed to measure the remaining search space. The whole product is still unresolved. [Exact decimal count and input](../benchmarks/local-midpoints.json)
