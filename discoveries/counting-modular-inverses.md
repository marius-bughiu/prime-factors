# One unweighted count for Euler's totient

Given only an $n$-bit integer $N>1$, count integer triples
$$
0\le x,y,k<N,\qquad xy=1+kN.
$$
The count is exactly $\varphi(N)$. Each unit $x$ has one inverse $y$ in the box and one quotient $k=(xy-1)/N$. Conversely, any accepted triple gives a unit and its inverse. The bounds guarantee $0\le k<N$ with no carry beyond the available product width. Lean verifies the bijection, the count and a bounded example for $N=15$. [Finite proof](../PrimeFactors/TotientTensor.lean)

For $N=pq$ with distinct primes, this gives $S=N+1-\varphi(N)$, then $p,q=(S\pm\sqrt{S^2-4N})/2$. This includes unbalanced prime pairs. For general $N$, Miller's Theorem 3 shows that a **single value** of $\varphi(N)$ suffices for deterministic polynomial-time recovery of the complete prime factorization under the extended Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet characters. His proof reuses the original multiple when it passes to divisors, so recursive splitting requires no additional count. The verified Dirichlet statements reviewed by this project supply the analytic hypothesis. The reduction to the network and the use of Miller's theorem are paper deductions. [Miller, Theorem 3 and preceding algorithm](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf#page=12) · [Scope of the Dirichlet result](../docs/research.md)

## The size of the counting problem

Use two copies of the parsimonious integer multiplier from Akshima et al. One computes the $2n$ bits of $xy$. The other multiplies $k$ by the pinned bits of $N$. A ripple incrementer enforces equality between $xy$ and $kN+1$, with its final overflow bit zero. Three linear-size comparator chains enforce $x,y,k<N$. A fixed input determines every new carry and comparison bit uniquely. Equality constraints copy wires and can make the network bipartite by subdivision. The resulting predicates are Boolean and no assignment weights are needed. The size remains $O(n)$ edges and nodes and the maximum degree is $O(n^{3/4})$. [Akshima et al., multiplier theorem](https://github.com/bochum-crypto/subexponential-ecdl/blob/main/subexponential-ecdl.pdf) · [Shunia's explicit encoding and uniformity conditions](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00711v1)

Since $\varphi(N)\le N<2^n$, a count modulo $2^n$ returns the exact natural number. For a sufficiently large constant multiple $s$ of $n$, the network meets the edge, degree, local evaluation and precision conditions of the quantitative Boolean tensor-contraction theorem. On paper this gives one deterministic unweighted contraction in $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ bit operations and polynomial space, followed by polynomial work under the Dirichlet hypothesis. Shunia already obtained the same general factoring exponent using repeated prefix counts. This variant identifies a single count, accepts arbitrary multiplicities, and separates the counting cost from the use of Dirichlet GRH. Its constants give no practical 2048-bit guarantee. [Quantitative contraction, Theorem D.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-reduction.tex) · [Shunia, Theorem 1](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00711v1)

The weighted one-multiplier construction computes the divisor sum $\sigma(N)$ instead. Bach, Miller and Shallit proved that the pair $(N,\sigma(N))$ gives a randomized polynomial-time algorithm for full factorization. For semiprimes both counts have deterministic elementary decoders. The two network descriptions make the cost of additional input wires, weighting, and postprocessing explicit.
