# Reading a common factor from two four-square representations

A single expression $N=a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2$ gives a way to label further expressions by ordinary remainders modulo $N$. If two labels agree at just one of the unknown primes, the gcd of their difference with $N$ gives the prime. For 667 the first three representations in the reference search produced labels 1 and 117. Their difference gave $\gcd(116,667)=29$ and $667=23\cdot29$. Finding sufficiently frequent matches on a large input remains open. ## Three gcds set up the coordinates

Assume $N=pq$ with distinct odd primes. Pollack and Treviño give an unconditional randomized algorithm to construct one four-square representation from $N$ in expected $O((\log N)^2/\log\log N)$ arithmetic operations on integers of $O(\log N)$ bits. They also give a simpler variant assuming Dirichlet GRH. These bounds concern finding one representation. [Pollack and Treviño](https://zenodo.org/records/10581444/files/sjs15.pdf)

For the seed $(a,b,c,d)$ test the gcd of $N$ with each of $a^2+b^2,a^2+c^2,a^2+d^2$, stopping at a proper factor or a unit. If all three vanished at $p$, their sum $2a^2+N$ would force $a,b,c,d$ all zero at $p$, contrary to $p^2\nmid N$. Cyclically rotate the imaginary coordinates to put a unit pivot $D=a^2+b^2$ first. Set
$$
u=(ac+bd)/D,\qquad v=(ad-bc)/D\pmod N.
$$
The two-square product identity gives $u^2+v^2=(c^2+d^2)/D=-1$. In these coordinates the Hamilton quaternion $\beta=(w,x,y,z)$ acts by the matrix
$$
 A(\beta)=
 \begin{pmatrix}w+uy-vz&-x+vy+uz\\ x+vy+uz&w-uy+vz\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplication of quaternions becomes matrix multiplication, and the determinant is $w^2+x^2+y^2+z^2$. Write $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_0&b_0\\c_0&d_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The sum $a_0+d_0$ and difference $c_0-b_0$ recover $2w$ and $2x$. With $h=a_0-d_0$ and $k=b_0+c_0$, the quantities $-uh-vk$ and $vh-uk$ recover $2y$ and $2z$. These linear formulas are invertible modulo odd $N$. At each input prime the matrix of a norm-$N$ quaternion therefore has rank one. [Lean pivot, matrix arithmetic, recovery and determinant proofs](../PrimeFactors/FourSquareLines.lean)

The rows of this matrix span one line at each prime. Inspect a row $(A,B)$. If either inspected entry has a proper gcd with $N$, return the factor. A unit $A$ gives the affine label $s=B/A$ in both fields. If $A=0\bmod N$ and $B$ is a unit, the line is at infinity at both primes. If both vanish, inspect the second row. Distinct finite labels $s,t$ give a common kernel at $p$ exactly when $s=t\bmod p$. Equivalently, the two quaternions share a right divisor of norm $p$ up to left association. This follows from the correspondence between local rank-one kernels and principal left ideals in the Hurwitz order. [Projective-line correspondence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.06259)

## Checking all pairs and counting the samples

For $k$ finite labels, discard exact duplicates modulo $N$ and build $F(X)=\prod_i(X-s_i)$. Evaluate $F'(s_i)=\prod_{j\ne i}(s_i-s_j)$ for all $i$ by a subproduct tree. This takes $O(M(k)\log k)$ ring operations if polynomial multiplication of degree $k$ costs $M(k)$, followed by $k$ integer gcds. If a gcd is $N$, fix that index and descend the tree of other factors until a leaf has a proper gcd. The sibling subtrees on the path from the chosen index partition the other factors. Distinctness modulo $N$ guarantees that a matching leaf gives a proper factor. The bounded benchmark uses direct pair gcds. [Multipoint evaluation over commutative rings](https://flintlib.org/doc/algorithms/polynomials/subproduct-tree.html)

The distribution of a four-square constructor's outputs need not be uniform. Starting from any one seed $\alpha$, choose a uniformly random quaternion $h\bmod N$ conditional on its norm being a unit. Compute a greatest common right divisor of $\alpha h$ and $N$ in the Hurwitz order. At each prime the kernel of $\alpha h$ is the image of a fixed line under a uniform inverse matrix. CRT makes the two lines independent. The left ideal generated by $\alpha h,N$ thus has index $p^2q^2$, since locally the matrices killing a given line form a two-dimensional space. Its generator has norm $N$ and those chosen kernels. A simpler source of uniform affine labels is to choose random ordinary residues directly.

For independent uniform *projective* lines the probability that a pair agrees at exactly one input prime is $(p+q)/((p+1)(q+1))$. For balanced primes the birthday threshold for a constant chance of a split is $\Theta(\sqrt p)$ samples, around $2^{512}$ for 1,024-bit primes. Batching saves pair checks. A product-dependent way to concentrate lines at one hidden prime would have to improve the sample count. Agol posed the quaternion-gcd approach in 2019 and Voight gave its random-pair obstruction. [Original question and answer](https://mathoverflow.net/a/337710) · [Hurwitz division and principal ideals](https://zenodo.org/records/10581444/files/sjs15.pdf)

The Lean example on 77 uses selected witnesses. Its trace-zero seed $(0,2,3,8)$ gives $u=4$, $v=37$. The witnesses $(0,3,2,8)$ and $(0,6,4,5)$ give slopes 9 and 20, and the checked prime factors are 7 and 11. On 667 the separate product-only benchmark tries nonnegative triples in lexicographic order, taking the integer square root of the remainder. After 248 triple tests it has the seed and the two colliding labels. On 60,491 the same rule uses 2,764 triple tests and finds 241 from the difference of 8,695 and 742.
