# Testing weighted powers at one prime at a time

One additional factor appeared when we relaxed the earlier weighted comparison. The previous method required two modular expressions to agree at **both** unknown primes. Here a match at just one prime suffices. On 24 independently drawn small semiprimes, this found 14 factorizations against 13 for the earlier power comparison. The weighted square search found 18. Neither 2048-bit control yielded a factor within its budget. [Fixed panel and exact counts](../research/local-gap-polynomial.json)

## What the powers test

Suppose $N=pq$ for distinct odd primes of known common bit width. Let $h=(N-1)/2$ and use the public base 2. For public weights $a,b$ and proposed weighted sum $s$, define $X=2^{s-(h+1)(a+b)}\pmod N$. For each public gap $d$, define $Y_d=2^{hd}\pmod N$. The original search looked up $X+X^{-1}$ among all $Y_d+Y_d^{-1}$ modulo $N$. At a single prime, equal traces mean $X=Y_d$ or $X=Y_d^{-1}$. A proper gcd of either ordinary difference with $N$ immediately identifies a prime factor. This includes accidental matches even when $s$ is not the true weighted sum. The same initial check $\gcd(Y_d^2-1,N)$ was available to every power method. [Original construction](comparing-weighted-powers-at-both-primes.md) · [Local trace identity](../PrimeFactors/LocalGapProducts.lean)

The first blind 11-bit draw illustrates the difference. For $N=1{,}433{,}779$, weights $(1,3)$ and coordinate $y=2075$ give $s=4150$ and $X=352359$. The matched gap was 12, while the weights' own gap was 2. The right power was 855882. The whole-modulus traces were 939170 and 1207935, so the earlier lookup missed. Both powers equal 222 modulo 1097. They give 776 and 1104 modulo 1307. Consequently $\gcd(855882-352359,N)=1097$ and $N=1097\cdot1307$. Lean checks the powers, inverses, trace difference, gcd and prime factors. Ordinary Fermat also splits this input in five square tests, ending at $1202^2-N=105^2$. ## One gcd for a group

For each coordinate $X$, multiply all differences $X-Y$ modulo $N$ over the distinct signed right powers $Y$. Drop $Y=X$, which contributes only the uninformative individual gcd $N$. One gcd of this product tests the whole row. A proper group gcd supplies a factor. If the gcd is $N$, each hidden prime divides at least one retained difference. No retained difference can be divisible by both, so the group necessarily contains two genuinely different local hits. Bisection locates a proper gcd in at most $\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ further gcds for $m$ retained differences. The implementation multiplies the tested halves directly. Finding one factor in this case also identifies the other factor known to occur in the group. [Proof of the saturated case](../PrimeFactors/LocalGapProducts.lean) · [Search implementation](../experiments/local_gap_polynomial.py)

The columns admit a different implementation. Form the polynomial $P(T)=\prod_X(T-X)$ over the distinct left residues. All signed right powers are on the public geometric progression $(2^h)^e$, where $e$ ranges over the selected positive and negative gaps. A coefficient shift followed by one convolution evaluates the whole interval between the extreme exponents. Only scheduled signed gaps are read. At shared keys $Y=X$, the ordinary evaluation is always zero. A second convolution, used only if there are shared keys, evaluates $P'$. At such a key $P'(Y)=\prod_{X\ne Y}(Y-X)$ even over the composite residue ring. Using this corrected value makes every remaining saturated cell a guaranteed proper-factor opportunity on refinement. In particular, an input with only a shared exact root and no local mismatches produces the empty product 1. [Ring and CRT proofs](../PrimeFactors/LocalGapProducts.lean) · [Earlier derivative construction](../PrimeFactors/TwelveCollisionBatch.lean)

A row takes at most one modular multiplication per signed right residue before its gcd. The column strategy pays to build the root polynomial once, store its coefficients and evaluate a full geometric grid. Sparse gap sets can waste work on unused exponents. Both strategies still require the scheduled weighted coordinates. The polynomial routines already used in the repository implement product-tree and geometric evaluation from David Harvey's factorization work. The measured comparison here concerns the new choice of keys and the treatment of exact matches. [Existing geometric evaluation](../experiments/weighted_stride_search.py) · [Earlier local batching and whole-gcd refinement](../experiments/wide_modulus.py) · [Harvey’s paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05450)

## Blind comparison

Before running the search, we fixed 12 independent draws at each of 11 and 15 bits, base 2, the multiplier cap 256, and four denominator coordinates per eligible interval. The two sides use independently labelled SHAKE256 sequences with seed `local-gap-polynomial-v1/<width>`. Equal prime pairs are skipped. Product bit length and method outcomes do not affect selection. Each search receives only the product, prime width and public budgets. We also ran plain ascending Fermat as a secondary comparison after seeing the initial results, giving it exactly as many midpoint tests as the weighted square method had distinct scheduled coordinates. Lean's aggregate certificate checks the 22 distinct prime pairs recovered by any method across the holdout and scale panels. [Draw rule](../experiments/affine_radix_holdout.py) · [Certificate generator and reproducible experiment](../experiments/local_gap_polynomial.py)

| Weighted row squares | 11 | 7 | 683 square tests | 1.08 ms |

| Polynomial plus derivative at shared roots | 9 | 5 | 329 gcds | 5.91 ms |
| Ordinary ascending Fermat | 7 | 4 | 683 square tests | 1.13 ms |

The four local policies returned precisely the same **set of individual proper divisors** on every input. The implementation checks that stronger statement against an untimed direct raw-coordinate oracle, including both orientations and repeated schedule entries. All four use the same cached power construction as the global lookup. Every method finishes its public budget. The counts for power methods include refinement gcds and exclude shared setup gcds, none of which split a holdout. CPU includes schedule construction, key preparation, all comparison work and counters. Entries sum the five-run per-case medians from one arm64 Python 3.9.6 run with rotating method order. First-hit times and stage-by-stage counters are available per input. Clock differences at this scale vary by run. [Measured results](../research/local-gap-polynomial.json)

On the prespecified 20-bit draw $861127154383$, limits 256 and 1024 gave no splits. At 4096 the weighted square search and every power method recovered $827473\cdot1040671$. There were 200 coordinates and 25 distinct signed powers. The local methods used 5,000 individual gcds, 201 row-product gcds or 25 polynomial gcds. Complete CPU was 2.28, 1.87 and 2.28 milliseconds respectively, with 2.05 ms for the derivative policy. No globally equal cell occurred on that row, so the derivative policy executed exactly the same mathematical operations as the plain polynomial policy. Weighted squares took 0.27 ms. [Scale comparison](../research/local-gap-polynomial.json)

The 2048-bit fresh and crossed controls used multiplier cap 64 and two coordinates per row. Fresh supplied 4 left and 3 signed right entries, crossed 12 left and 5 signed right entries. Direct local comparisons gave 12 and 60 gcds equal to 1. The polynomial used 3 and 5 column gcds, all equal to 1, and took about 36 and 73 ms end to end on this run. The corresponding direct local searches took 35 and 67 ms. Key construction dominated both. Each full public multiplier limit has 206 decimal digits. These short runs establish complete agreement within the tested prefix and give no practical factoring time for the full search. [Full-size budgets, counts and timings](../research/local-gap-polynomial.json)
