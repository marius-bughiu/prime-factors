# Can multiplying a giant exponent pay for a smaller baby table?

Multiplying a public giant exponent can turn every usable local quadratic image into a square image. The longer list of possible exponent labels can consume the entire saving. We prove a bound that still applies if each giant keeps only the labels divisible by its own multiplier after translation, clips them to its own span, and shares duplicate labels for free.

**Result.** Suppose $t$ distinct odd sieve primes have product $M$. Each of the $2^t$ leading-sign patterns must be supported for an input index in $[0,H)$, and $H/M\ge 2^t$. Give each pattern a nonzero integer multiplier $w$, arbitrary local translations, and an arbitrary integer offset. Require its entire Cartesian local image over that index interval. If every $w$ is coprime to $M$, the number of distinct output exponent labels is at least
$$
 H\rho\exp\!\left[-O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{t\log\log(3+t)}{\log(2+t)}}\right)\right],
 \qquad
 \rho=\prod_{\ell\mid M}\frac{3\ell+\chi_\ell(-1)}{4\ell}.
$$
The ordinary translated table has $H\rho+O(M\rho)$ labels. Thus the menu cannot improve its exponential-in-$t$ saving in this model. The lower bound permits multipliers of arbitrarily large magnitude. For multipliers of height $N^{o(1)}$, even multipliers sharing primes with $M$ retain the same exponential constant in the balanced short-basis parameter regime. The proof below uses a theorem on sums of greatest common divisors. Lean checks the finite intersection and second-moment bounds. ## Exact alignment and collision recovery

At a usable prime, Stănică's local leading coefficient is $aN\sigma^{-3}$. The public lattice relation $b\sigma^2=aN\bmod\ell$ identifies it with $b/\sigma$. Take $w=b\sigma$ modulo the product of the usable primes. The new leading coefficient is $b^2$ at each of them. Completing the square and translating its center then aligns all those images with the set $Q_\ell$ of squares including zero. The least positive representative for this particular CRT prescription can be as large as the period. If instead an integer menu must supply the prescribed Legendre signs for all patterns, each multiplier supplies just one vector of signs, so exact alignment requires at least $2^t$ distinct signatures. Permitting up to $h$ mismatches replaces this count by the necessary covering inequality $K\sum_{j=0}^h\binom tj\ge 2^t$ for a menu of $K$ signatures. [Source, Lemma 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06580v1#S3) · [Checked coefficient identity](../PrimeFactors/MultiplierImages.lean)

For a collision $\alpha^i=v\bmod p$, the giant $\alpha^c v^w$ collides with $\alpha^{wi+c}$ modulo $p$. Negative powers are available for units, and an integer translation can put the labels in a nonnegative span. On an exact match modulo $N$, decode $(e-c)/w$, check integrality and the index interval, and apply the original factor test. Taking a power need not preserve inequality. For a genuine giant, certifying $\operatorname{ord}_p(\alpha)$ greater than the width of the complete tested exponent span forces any exact match to have the true label. A proper difference gcd yields a factor directly. This can enlarge the order required in the original algorithm. Enumerating a preselected menu costs at least its length; computing all its signatures directly takes $O(tK)$ local character evaluations. The explicit $b\sigma$ choice avoids the menu search. Computing the giant transformations, local translations and CRT combinations still costs work per giant. The lower bound concerns the output labels alone. [Stănică, Lemmas 3.9–3.11](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06580v1#S3) · [Earlier reflection and exact-match check](reflected-image-sieve.md)

## Proof for unit multipliers

Let $U_\ell=(3\ell+\chi_\ell(-1))/4$, $U=\prod_\ell U_\ell$ and $V=H/M$. Retain one row per input sign pattern. At least half of these rows have multipliers of the same sign. Work with this group, so distinct signed multipliers have distinct absolute values. For a fixed multiplier $w$, let $P_w$ be its set of assigned sign patterns and $A_w$ the union of those rows' output residue boxes modulo $M$. Scaling by a unit permutes the two leading signs at each coordinate. Hence
$$
 |A_w|\ \ge\ U\,|P_w|/2^t .
$$
To prove this, compress arbitrary local translations to an optimal common pair of translates for each coordinate, using the coordinate-fiber argument. A point sampled uniformly from the product of the canonical local unions forces a sign at exclusive points and allows either sign at intersection points. Choose each ambiguous sign fairly. The resulting signs are independent and fair, because each of the two local images has the same size. If the chosen pattern belongs to the assigned set after scaling, the point belongs to its union. This gives the inequality. [Finite coordinate compression](coupled-image-sieve.md)

Now fix an output residue $x\bmod M$. For each of the $n$ distinct multipliers with $x\in A_w$, choose just one supporting row. Its affine labels $wi+c$ at this residue form a segment of an arithmetic progression of spacing $M|w|$. It has at least $\lfloor V\rfloor$ entries and span at most $H|w|$. This uses the complete-image requirement on the row and the invertibility of $w$ modulo $M$. The intersection of two such segments at absolute multipliers $u,v$ has spacing a multiple of $M\operatorname{lcm}(u,v)$ and span at most $H\min(u,v)$. Therefore its cardinality is at most
$$
 1+V\frac{\gcd(u,v)}{\max(u,v)}
 \ \le\ 1+V\frac{\gcd(u,v)}{\sqrt{uv}} .
$$
The bound holds for arbitrary offsets even when the segments lie very far apart. The diagonal case is also valid. [Finite progression count](../PrimeFactors/MultiplierImages.lean)

For distinct positive integers $u_1,\ldots,u_n$, Bondarenko and Seip prove the uniform estimate
$$
 \sum_{i,j=1}^n\frac{\gcd(u_i,u_j)}{\sqrt{u_i u_j}}
 \le n\exp\!\left(O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n\,\log\log\log n}{\log\log n}}\right)\right).
$$
Use a fixed larger constant for small $n$. As $n\le2^t$, write $K_t=\exp(O(\sqrt{t\log\log(3+t)/\log(2+t)}))$ for a uniform upper bound on the sum divided by $n$. If $n>0$, the total number of pairwise overlaps of the selected progressions is at most $n^2+VnK_t$. Their total length counted with multiplicity is at least $nV/2$. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds their union below by the square of this length divided by the overlaps. Since $V\ge2^t\ge n$, this gives at least $nV/(4(1+K_t))$ distinct exponent labels at $x$. Sum over $x$, using $\sum_x n=\sum_w |A_w|\ge U/2$, to obtain the stated result. This argument charges the separate clipped progressions and never assumes a complete period of their joint least common multiple. [Bondarenko–Seip, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0249) · [Finite union inequality](../PrimeFactors/MultiplierImages.lean)

## When a multiplier contains sieve primes

For the balanced parameters with $m=N^{1/10+o(1)}$ and $m_0=N^{1/10+o(1)}$, $t\asymp\log N/\log\log N$, $\min_{\ell\mid M}\log\ell\asymp\log\log N$, and $H/M=N^{1/10+o(1)}$. If all $|w|\le N^{o(1)}$, each multiplier shares at most $k=o(t)$ primes with $M$. Partition the rows by that set of bad primes and by the sign of $w$. There are at most $2\sum_{j=0}^k\binom tj=\exp(o(t))$ parts. Retain one of maximum size. Write $g$ for the product of its bad primes, $k'$ for their count, and $M'=M/g$. On the other coordinates all its multipliers are units.

At a fixed supported residue $x\bmod M'$, one row per multiplier has at least $\lfloor H/M\rfloor\prod_{\ell\mid g}\frac{\ell+1}{2}$ labels. Indeed, there is one possible original residue at each good coordinate, and $(\ell+1)/2$ original choices at each bad coordinate. This gives at least $(H/M')/2^{k'+1}$ distinct integer labels per row. Ignore the bad restrictions for the pairwise intersection upper bound, replacing $M$ by $M'$ in the preceding argument. Squaring the lower bound on row size costs a factor of at most $4^{k'}$ beyond its original constant. In the estimate for $|A_w|$, projection can identify at most $2^{k'}$ input sign patterns, and the denominator on the good coordinates is now $2^{t-k'}$. Thus $|A_w|\ge(\prod_{\ell\mid M'}U_\ell)|P_w|/2^t$ still holds. The chosen part contains $2^t\exp(-o(t))$ rows, and $\prod_{\ell\mid M'} (U_\ell/\ell)\ge\rho$. Together these facts give the lower bound $H\rho\exp(-o(t))$. A common $o(t)$ set of degenerate input coordinates can likewise be left free or omitted. The argument for arbitrary-height multipliers was established above only for units.

The same exponential-constant obstruction holds in the wider-modulus regime $H/M\ge2$ if $\min_{\ell\mid M}\log\ell\to\infty$ and each multiplier meets $o(t)$ sieve primes. In each group, omit up to $\lceil t\log 2/\min_{\ell\mid M}\log\ell\rceil=o(t)$ additional good primes until $H/M'\ge2^t$. The original input window still contains $\lfloor H/M\rfloor\ge H/(2M)$ complete periods. Count the omitted source constraints inside those periods as above. This changes the lower bound by $\exp(-o(t))$, although its sharper displayed subleading error need not persist.

## Which signs the shortest vectors actually produce

Fix a geometry cell and a residue $\sigma\bmod m$ with the canonical locally usable shortest weights $(a,b)$. At independently chosen primes of the squarefree modulus $m$, replace $\sigma$ by $-\sigma$ using CRT. This preserves $N\sigma^{-2}$ and hence the entire lattice. If the tie rule uses the lattice parameter and geometry alone, it returns the same weights. The leading sign of $aN\sigma^{-3}$ flips exactly at the chosen primes congruent to $3\bmod4$. All sign patterns on those usable coordinates occur in this public lattice orbit; signs at primes congruent to $1\bmod4$ stay fixed. At a latter prime, $abN=(b\sigma)^2$ locally and the leading sign is $\chi_\ell(b\sigma)$, so it is positive exactly when $abN$ is a fourth power. This statement concerns the enumerated giant records. Only one residue represents the unknown factor. The full-pattern lower bound for a mixed set of primes requires evidence about the other signs or an explicit commitment to support every sign box. [Underlying lattice and leading coefficient](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06580v1#S3)

For a finite example, use one input period $0\le i<77$ at primes 7 and 11. The multipliers $1,2,3,6$ have the four distinct signatures and send the corresponding unshifted image boxes to square images. A full period of the aligned square residues contains only $4\cdot6=24$ labels, compared with 40 in the ordinary two-sign table. Listing these square residues over the enlarged span $[0,6\cdot77)$ takes 144 labels. Keeping only labels divisible by each multiplier and clipping to its individual span reduces the union to **60**, still above 40. Lean checks both table counts. [Kernel-checked example](../PrimeFactors/MultiplierImages.lean)

The obstruction applies to one affine exponent map for each complete local image row, with a common original index length. A portfolio of different maps that splits the *indices of a single row*, a shorter support proved for a particular product, or an implicit algebraic computation of many powers would require a separate analysis.
