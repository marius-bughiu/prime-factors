# What an approximate quadratic class number buys

Let $N=pq$ with odd distinct primes $p<q\le2p$. First suppose $N\equiv3\pmod4$, so $D=-N$ is a fundamental discriminant. The last section handles $N\equiv1\pmod4$. The primitive quadratic character $\chi_D(n)=(D/n)$ is available from the input by the Kronecker–Jacobi algorithm. Dirichlet's class number formula connects it to the finite group $G$ of proper classes of primitive positive definite binary quadratic forms of discriminant $D$:
$$
h=|G|=\frac{\sqrt N}{\pi}L(1,\chi_D).
$$
On this input family, a certified interval containing $h$ of width $W$ gives a factor search using $O(\sqrt W)$ form compositions per auxiliary prime, plus logarithmic initialization. Finding the right prime needs only the Dirichlet GRH statement used elsewhere in this project. Estimating the class number and searching the interval by class-group operations is the classical approach of Shanks. [Shanks](https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/020/0316385) · [Class number formula and GRH bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3595#page=4) · [Released Dirichlet statements](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab/src/Statements.lean)

## Which class yields the factors?

Write $p^*=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p$ and similarly $q^*$. Since $D=p^*q^*$ has two prime discriminant factors, genus theory gives $|G/G^2|=2$. The square map on the finite abelian group $G$ therefore has a kernel of size two. Write a reduced form as $[a,b,c]$, meaning $ax^2+bxy+cy^2$ with $|b|\le a\le c$ and $b\ge0$ at equality. A symmetric form $[a,b,a]$ is equivalent to its inverse $[a,-b,a]$ by exchanging the variables with one sign change. In this case
$$
a=(p+q)/4,\qquad b=(q-p)/2.
$$
These are integers because the odd primes have opposite residues modulo 4. The form has discriminant $-N$ and is primitive since a common divisor of its coefficients would have its square divide $N$. Balance gives $0<b\le a$, and $a>1$ distinguishes it from the principal reduced form. Thus this is the unique nonidentity class of order two. Once its reduced coefficients are found, $2a-b=p$ and $2a+b=q$. [Genus characters and the principal genus theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.1228#page=5) · [Lean coefficient identities](../PrimeFactors/ClassNumberWindow.lean)

For a rational odd prime $\ell\nmid N$ satisfying $\chi_D(\ell)=1$, find an odd $b$ with $b^2\equiv D\pmod{4\ell}$ and form $[\ell,b,(b^2-D)/(4\ell)]$. Brute force up to $2\ell$ suffices for this small auxiliary prime. Reduce it to obtain a class $g$. Its genus sign is $(p^*/\ell)$, which also equals $(q^*/\ell)$. If both signs are $-1$, $g$ has even order. The algorithm tries split primes using the public $\chi_D$ test. It can recognize success by the extracted factor without computing either hidden genus sign. [Genus-character construction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.1228#page=5) · [Form reduction and arithmetic](https://flintlib.org/doc/algorithms/number-theory/binary-quadratic-forms.html)

An explicit search bound follows from the least-prime-in-a-coset theorem. On the units modulo $2N$, the map given by the two Legendre signs is onto $\{\pm1\}^2$ by CRT and quadratic reciprocity. The desired signs form one coset of its index-four kernel. For $2N\ge20000$, Dirichlet GRH gives an odd prime in the coset at most
$$
\max\left\{10^9,\left(3\log(2N)+15+\tfrac52(\log\log(2N))^2\right)^2\right\}.
$$
For a 2048-bit input the maximum here is one billion. Scanning eligible primes may stop as soon as the gcd or form coefficients give a split. The bound concerns one nonprincipal-genus class. A theorem that small prime ideals generate the *entire* class group typically assumes GRH for a larger family of L-functions. [Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595v3#S1.SS2) · [Grenié–Molteni on class-group generators](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02430)

## How to use the interval

Suppose $1\le L\le h\le U$, set $W=U-L+1$ and $m=\lceil\sqrt W\rceil$. For each chosen $g$, store $g^{-j}$ for $0\le j<m$. Compare $g^{L+im}$ with the table for $0\le i<\lceil W/m\rceil$. The division algorithm applied to $h-L$ guarantees a match because $g^h=1$. Any match gives a positive exponent $e=L+im+j$ with $g^e=1$. Even an exponent in the overhang of the last block is usable. Keeping one index per duplicate table entry suffices.

Write $e=2^v u$ with $u$ odd. If the genus sign is $-1$, then $g^u\ne1$. Square $g^u$ repeatedly until its next square is the identity. The last nonidentity class is the one yielding $p,q$. This works with *any* matched exponent, so a collision at a proper multiple of $\operatorname{ord}(g)$ need not identify $h$. The finite Lean theorems verify the table match and this powering argument. Binary-form composition, genus theory and the class number formula are used on paper. Searching costs $O(\sqrt W+\log U)$ group operations per prime and $O(\sqrt W)$ table entries. Sorted entry comparisons contribute logarithmic overhead. [Lean window and involution proofs](../PrimeFactors/ClassNumberWindow.lean)

## Truncation cost and failed shortcuts

An absolute error $\eta$ in $L(1,\chi_D)$ gives an interval of length $2\sqrt N\,\eta/\pi$ for $h$. Since $h$ is even, $\eta<\pi/\sqrt N$ suffices for unique rounding to an even integer. This is about half the input bit length in absolute precision, roughly 1024 bits at the motivating scale. Efficient individual Jacobi symbols do not themselves provide that accuracy.

For a concrete estimate, let $P_T=\prod_{\ell\le T}(1-\chi_D(\ell)/\ell)^{-1}$ over primes. Under Dirichlet GRH, the prime-character sum through $y$ is $O(\sqrt y\log(Ny))$. Partial summation bounds the tail of $\sum\chi_D(\ell)/\ell$ beyond $T$ by $O(\log(NT)/\sqrt T)$. Higher prime powers contribute $O(1/T)$ there. This gives the same $O(\log(NT)/\sqrt T)$ absolute error for $\log L(1,\chi_D)-\log P_T$. For a bound below 1 this implies relative error of the same order in the L-value. Also $L(1,\chi_D)=O(\log N)$ by summing through one period and partial summation using the zero sum over a complete period. A certified constant in the tail bound, and downward and upward rounding of the numerical operations, are needed for an actual finite interval. [Taheri–Saleh, Lemma 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.19610#page=5)

Suppressing logarithmic factors, calculating the prime product costs $T$ and gives $W=O(1+\sqrt N/\sqrt T)$ up to those factors. The table search then costs about $N^{1/4}T^{-1/4}$ in the resulting upper bound. Balancing the two terms at $T\asymp N^{1/5}$ recovers the classical fifth-root scale. For a 2048-bit input that power alone is about $2^{410}$. This calculation is an upper-bound tradeoff for these estimates. It leaves open other ways of computing the requisite character value or producing a shorter interval. [Shanks' factorization method](https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/020/0316385) · [Lenstra's analysis of quadratic-field computations](https://public.csusm.edu/ssharif/crypto/Lenstra82-QuadraticRegulators.pdf)

Two simpler conjectures fail on balanced inputs:

- **Rounding a short Euler product.** For $N=24511=127\cdot193$, truncation at $T=\lfloor(\log N)^2\rfloor=102$ uses no ramified prime. Among the primes up to 102, exactly $2,5,29,31,53,89$ have positive sign. The resulting rational product is $20930424198440539677936760249/19589141954544492752142336000$. Multiplication by $\sqrt N/\pi$ gives approximately $53.247$, with nearest even integer 54. Counting reduced forms gives the true class number 50. Lean checks the finite count over $1\le a\le90$, the full range since $3a^2\le N$.
- **Using the first split prime.** For $N=391=17\cdot23$, the class number is 14. The first split prime 2 has genus sign $(17/2)=+1$, so its classes lie in the principal genus of order 7. No power of either can reach an involution. At prime 5 both signs are $-1$. Its even-order class reaches $[10,3,10]$, giving 17 and 23. Lean checks the reduced-form counts and the endpoint arithmetic. [Finite calculations](../PrimeFactors/ClassNumberWindow.lean)

## The second residue class of the input

When $N\equiv1\pmod4$, use $D=-4N$. This has three prime discriminant factors $p^*,q^*,-4$. The public character, the class number formula with $\sqrt{|D|}$, and the interval search work as before. Now $G[2]$, the set of classes whose square is the identity, has four elements. It includes the reduced primitive form $[p,0,q]$ of discriminant $-4N$. Its leading coefficient is the smaller prime. Other nonidentity classes can encode the known factor 2 of the discriminant, so obtaining all three involutions is sufficient.

By CRT on units modulo $4N$, the characters $\chi_{p^*},\chi_{q^*},\chi_{-4}$ give all eight sign triples, and a split prime has sign product $+1$. The index-eight case of the same least-prime-in-a-coset bound gives split primes with prescribed signatures $(-,+,-)$ and $(+,-,-)$. The bound for either is $\max\{10^9,(7\log(4N)+27+\tfrac52(\log\log(4N))^2)^2\}$ for $4N\ge20000$. Their form classes map to independent elements of $G/G^2$. To locate them without knowing $p,q$, try all split primes up to the bound. Find an annihilator $e$ for each using the interval table, and replace that form by its power to the odd part of $e$. This kills its odd-order component and preserves all genus signs. [Least-prime bound](https://arxiv.org/html/1309.3595v3#S1.SS2) · [Genus theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.1228)

Try pairs of the resulting elements. For a pair $x,y$ with independent genus signs, relabel so $x$ has the larger order $2^a$, measurable by successive squaring. Write $i_x=x^{2^{a-1}}$. Start with $z=y$, whose independence puts it outside $\langle x\rangle$. If $z$ has order $2^b$ and $z^{2^{b-1}}=i_x$, replace $z$ by $zx^{2^{a-b}}$. Its order drops by at least a factor of two, and multiplying by a power of $x$ preserves its coset modulo $\langle x\rangle$. Thus for the independent pair the procedure must reach a nonidentity $i_z=z^{2^{b-1}}$ different from $i_x$. For other pairs one can skip a pair as soon as $z$ becomes the identity. Reduce $i_x,i_z,i_xi_z$ and check their coefficients for proper divisors of $N$. For the independent pair these are all three nonidentity involutions, including $[p,0,q]$. Lean proves the order-drop and coset-preservation step. The interpretation of the resulting two generators in the class group is on paper. [Lean cancellation theorem](../PrimeFactors/ClassNumberWindow.lean)

With $s$ trial split primes one needs $s$ interval searches and at most $O(s^2(\log U)^2)$ further group operations for the pairs. The prime cutoff grows as $O((\log N)^2)$, so the soft fifth-root bound is unchanged. This extension covers all balanced odd semiprimes with distinct prime factors and still depends on obtaining a sufficiently narrow certified class-number interval.
