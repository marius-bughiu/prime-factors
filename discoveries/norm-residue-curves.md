# Recovering integer points from a short residue

For an ellipse $x^2+dy^2=T$, a single residue of one coordinate and a choice between two signs can locate an integer point. The modulus needs roughly a quarter of the bits of $T$ when the degree of the auxiliary equation grows. With no supplied residue we must try the classes ourselves. This gives a second way to reduce the earlier $T^{1/3}$ search to $T^{1/4}$ times a polynomial in the bit length, still far too much work at 2,048 bits. This construction uses local congruences and lattice reduction, drawing on the determinant method. A separate construction divides the ellipse into short angular sectors and achieves the same asymptotic bound. ## An integer equation for a whole residue class

Write the chart as $Ax^2+By^2=T$, with $(A,B)=(1,d)$ or $(d,1)$. Choose an odd prime $\ell$ not dividing $2dT$. Enumerate the solutions modulo $\ell$ and use the chart for which $y$ is a unit. Take $Q=\ell^h$, positive degree $D$, $m=2D$, $s=2D+1$. A class is specified by $x=u\bmod Q$ and $y=v\bmod\ell$. Put $H=x-u$. Work modulo $Q^m$. Lift the solution $v$ at $x=u$ to that precision by Newton's rule. Write the ordinate as $Y(H)=v+c_1 H+\cdots+c_{m-1} H^{m-1}$. Coefficient comparison in $B Y(H)^2=T-A(u+H)^2$ determines each $c_j$ since $2Bv$ is invertible. Terms containing $H^m$ vanish at every point of this class. The actual ordinate and the series have the same square after multiplication by $B$. Their sum is a unit at $\ell$, so the ordinates agree modulo $Q^m$. [Lift, cancellation and vanishing lemmas in Lean](../PrimeFactors/NormResidueCurve.lean)

Take as basis $1,x,\ldots,x^D,y,xy,\ldots,x^{D-1}y$. Substitute the two local expansions into an integer linear combination $F=P(x)+yG(x)$ of this basis. Require the coefficient of $H^j$ to vanish modulo $Q^{m-j}$ for $0\le j<m$. Each condition increases the index of the integer coefficient lattice by at most its modulus. The full index is at most $Q^{s(s-1)/2}$. Each retained $F$ vanishes modulo $Q^m$ at every integer point in the class. The implementation constructs the full kernel one congruence at a time. All pivot gcds are powers of the same prime. The smallest gcd divides the other row images, so subtracting multiples of its row clears the other images. Multiplying the pivot row by its additive order gives a basis of the new kernel. [Exact implementation](../experiments/norm_residue_curves.py)

Bound each monomial in absolute value by its weight $w_i$, using $|x|\le X, |y|\le Y$ from the ellipse. Scale the coefficient lattice by those column weights. The LLL bound gives a nonzero coefficient vector whose weighted sum of absolute values is at most
$$\sqrt{s}\,2^{(s-1)/4} Q^{(s-1)/2}\left(\prod_i w_i\right)^{1/s}.$$
Choose $Q$ so this is strictly below $Q^m$. Then every integral point in the class satisfies the exact equation $F=0$. We check this strict inequality on the returned vector as well. The polynomial $BP(x)^2+(Ax^2-T)G(x)^2$ is nonzero. If it vanished identically, evaluating it at all sufficiently large integers would force both $P$ and $G$ to vanish there, hence everywhere. Isolate its integer roots on the progression $x=u+Qj$ and test the original ellipse exactly. [LLL](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/9304) · [Elimination and root isolation](norm-auxiliary-curves.md)

The same eliminant includes both signs of $y$. Allowing signed $x$ covers the classes $u$ and $-u$ in one reduction. We use one representative from each pair of residues and test only positive $x$ when $u=0$. This handles points on the same prime-field branch as well as their reflections.

## Number of classes

Put $R=\lceil\sqrt T\rceil$. The total basis degree is $D(D+1)$, so $\prod_i w_i\le R^{D(D+1)}$. A sufficient strict cutoff is
$$Q>\sqrt{2}\,s^{1/(s-1)} R^{(D+1)/(2D+1)}.$$
Passing to the next prime power costs at most a factor $\ell$. There are $O(\ell)$ initial conic points since each abscissa has at most two ordinates modulo $\ell$, and each has $Q/\ell$ choices of free residue. The least prime avoiding $2dT$ is $O(1+\log(2dT))$ by the prime number theorem for the product of consecutive primes. All inputs to the lattice algorithm have polynomial bit size in $D$ and $\log(2dT)$, including the precision $Q^{2D}$. At fixed $D=2$ the exponent of $T$ in the class count is $3/10$. Taking $D=\max(1,\lceil\log_2 R\rceil)$ bounds $R^{1/(4D+2)}$ by $2^{1/4}$, leaving $T^{1/4}$ times polynomial logarithmic factors. Standard polynomial-time integer root isolation or rational polynomial factorization suffices within each class. For the factoring application $d$ and the multiplier in $T=kN$ are bounded by polynomials in $\log N$. The argument uses the published LLL bound on paper. The Lean file checks its local congruence consequences.

For $N=1{,}900{,}014{,}700{,}027$ the degree-two search took 3,094 classes to find both revealing points on $x^2+2y^2=N$. The previous line search took 10,600 blocks to split the same input. Lattice reduction makes the classes more expensive. On the full 41-bit ellipse the residue search was slower in elapsed time. The 2,048-bit comparison stopped after two classes with the entire cofactor unresolved.
