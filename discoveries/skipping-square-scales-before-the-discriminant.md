# Which square-root guesses can we skip?

For $4{,}757=67\times71$, the square-root search first succeeds at guess 1,155. A precomputed table brings the number of scale-root calculations down from 1,155 to 740. The factor and stopping index stay the same. The table works because the **first** successful scale is squarefree for every balanced semiprime in range. ## A fixed table for every product

Skip multiples of $4,9,25$. In each block of 900 consecutive guesses with aligned endpoints, 576 remain. The Chinese remainder theorem explains the count: the three square moduli are pairwise coprime, leaving $3\times8\times24=576$ allowed residue combinations. Lean verifies the exact count including a final partial block, and proves the sieved search has a hit through a given cap precisely when the original balanced-input search has one. A later, nonsquarefree scale can succeed even though it is skipped. It always has an earlier squarefree success. [Finite count and preservation of the first hit](../PrimeFactors/JacobiSquareScaleSieve.lean)

On 4,757 a cap of 900 left the number unresolved in both runs. Scale roots fell from 900 to 576 and discriminant roots from 849 to 542. Continuing to the winning scale 1,155 reduced the discriminant roots from 1,104 to 706. Building the reusable table cost 2,175 small divisibility checks once. The successful run then read 740 entries. These are counts of arithmetic operations; their costs differ. ## The product can exclude additional guesses

At $N=3{,}713$, the **first successful scale must be odd**. The product tells us $128\mid N-1$. If the winning squarefree scale $t$ were even, $128$ would divide $t^7$, so the following theorem would force $128$ to divide the root $y$ in $(p-1)(q-1)=ty^2$. Even $128^2$ already exceeds $N$. This excludes all even scales before taking a square root. The same argument with $29\mid N-1$ excludes multiples of 29 because $29^3>N$. [Lean support and height bounds](../PrimeFactors/JacobiSquareScaleGcd.lean)

In general, take any $d\mid N-1$ whose primes all divide the squarefree candidate $t$. If $t$ gives the true sum, then **$d\mid y$ and $td^2<N$**. To prove this, write $p-1=ga$, $q-1=gb$ with $a,b$ coprime. The integer $(N-1)/g=a+b+gab$ is coprime to $ab$. A successful squarefree scale divides $ab$, and $y$ is a multiple of $g$. Thus every prime power of $N-1$ supported on $t$ is already present in $y$. The public height test $td^2<N$ can reject a guess before computing $y$. If it passes, $d\mid y$ gives a second inexpensive arithmetic check once the scale root is available. [Lean derivation](../PrimeFactors/JacobiSquareScaleGcd.lean)

The experiment took the full powers in $N-1$ of primes through 41 and used the same balanced-sum interval in both runs. At 3,713 it reduced scale roots from 574 to 371 and left two discriminant roots in both runs. It used 18 setup divisions and 1,148 small-modulus tests on candidate scales. At 4,757 the counts fell from 740 to 524 scale roots and from four to three discriminant roots, at the cost of 17 setup divisions, 2,220 candidate modulus tests and 58 further root modulus tests. At 221 it saved zero square roots. The inputs were chosen to exercise the arithmetic checks; elapsed-time savings have not been established.
