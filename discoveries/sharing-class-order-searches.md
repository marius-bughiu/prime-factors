# Sharing work between quadratic class searches

On 32 fresh products of two 17-bit primes, two versions of a quadratic-form search found the same 31 factorizations. Carrying information from one search into the next cut the number of form multiplications from 86,408 to 44,221. Ordinary trial division found all 32 faster. The work saved has a mathematical explanation that also applies to larger inputs. The first search can still be enormous. [Paired measurements](../research/class-order-holdout.json) · [Lean proofs](../PrimeFactors/SharedClassOrders.lean)

## What the product tells us

Suppose $N=pq$ for distinct odd primes $p<q\le2p$. The product alone specifies a finite abelian group of reduced quadratic forms, using discriminant $\Delta=-N$ when $N\equiv3\pmod4$ and $\Delta=-4N$ otherwise. Write $h$ for the number of classes. If $\Delta=-N$, exactly one nonidentity class squares to the identity. Its reduced form is $[a,b,a]$, with $2a-b=p$ and $2a+b=q$. If $\Delta=-4N$, there are three nonidentity classes that square to the identity. One is $[p,0,q]$. So we can recognize success by taking a gcd of a reduced coefficient or a specified combination of coefficients with $N$. [Genus theory and both extraction rules](quadratic-class-number.md)

For every small split prime $r$ we construct a form $g$ from a public square root of $\Delta\bmod 4r$. Each $g^h$ is the identity. Starting with an interval $[L,U]$ known to contain $h$, the earlier algorithm searched this entire interval again for each $g$. We can reuse information from a failed seed. For 24,511 the first form has order 25. Thus $25\mid h$. For the second form the calculation can start with its 25th power and search for $h/25$. Its involution reveals $127\times193$. This takes 46 form compositions, compared with 72 for independent windows on those seeds. [Benchmark settings and outputs](../benchmarks/class-orders.json)

## Why the next search gets cheaper

Keep a positive integer $D\mid h$ that annihilates all forms already processed, initially 1. For a fresh $g$, form $x=g^D$. If $x=1$ we already have an annihilator. Otherwise search for exponents $e$ with $x^e=1$ between $\lceil L/D\rceil$ and $\lfloor U/D\rfloor$. At least $h/D$ works.

* If exactly one exponent works, it must be $h/D$. Now we know $h$ and have an annihilator for every other seed.
* If two consecutive exponents work, their difference is the exact order of $x$, since the working exponents are exactly the multiples of that order. An exact order $t$ also arises if the baby table itself repeats. In either case $Dt$ divides $h$ and annihilates $g$ and every earlier seed. Since $x\ne1$, $D$ at least doubles.

For a quotient window of width $w$, take $m=\lceil\sqrt w\rceil$. Store $x^j$ for $0\le j<m$. If any of the first $m$ positive powers is the identity, the first such exponent is the order. Otherwise the table has distinct entries. In consecutive blocks beginning at $A$, look up $x^{-A}$ and accept a matching baby only when $A+j$ is still inside the window. Each block has at most one hit because the order exceeds $m$. This returns the first two consecutive hits in increasing order, or certifies a unique hit after exhausting the interval. The very first match already suffices to attempt extraction on the current seed. The enlargement is needed only if that attempt fails. [Group identities in Lean](../PrimeFactors/SharedClassOrders.lean)

Write $W=U-L+1$ and let $s$ be the number of seeds. At the $i$th search starting from $i=0$, $D_i\ge2^i$ and $w_i\le(W-1)/D_i+1$. Thus $\sum_i \lceil\sqrt{w_i}\rceil\le(2+\sqrt2)\sqrt W+2s$. Each paid search takes at most two table lengths in multiplications plus initial binary powering. All interval searches together take $O(\sqrt W+s\log U)$ group operations, in place of $O(s\sqrt W+s\log U)$. The largest table still requires $O(\sqrt W)$ entries. This counts group operations separately from computing the starting interval, finding the split primes, and storing or comparing their forms. Reusing known element orders to compute a set exponent is classical. Sutherland’s Algorithm 8.2 gives the same multiplicative update. Here the quotient window can supply either an exact element order or the class number itself. [Sutherland, §8.3](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/sutherland-phd.pdf#page=134) · [Shanks's class-group method](https://doi.org/10.1090/pspum/020/0316385)

## One pivot suffices for the second discriminant

For every seed with annihilator $e$, let $u$ be its odd part. The class $g^u$ has power-of-two order and retains every genus sign of $g$. We test its last nonidentity square immediately, and keep the chain when that test fails. Choose a single class $x$ of largest order $2^a$ among the retained chains. If two trial seeds have independent genus signs, some retained class $z$ lies outside $\langle x\rangle$, since the signs of elements in this cyclic subgroup span at most one direction. Its order is $2^b\le2^a$. Whenever $z$ and $x$ have the same last nonidentity square, replace $z$ by $zx^{2^{a-b}}$. This keeps $z$ outside the subgroup and at least halves its order. Eventually we obtain a different involution. It, the pivot's involution, and their product give all three nonidentity members of the 2-torsion. The search also handles a class inside the pivot's subgroup by stopping when it reaches the identity. A single pivot takes $O(s\log^2 U)$ further multiplications instead of trying all pairs. [Induction in Lean](../PrimeFactors/SharedClassOrders.lean)

The earlier small-prime argument still applies. Dirichlet GRH bounds the prime needed for a nontrivial genus sign at $-N$, and the primes with two independent signs at $-4N$. The bounded numerical experiment uses a smaller, fixed menu with no completeness guarantee. The reuse and pivot arguments themselves apply to any supplied menu. ## Computing from the product alone

The code uses a coarse unconditional interval. The identity, distinct reduced seed forms and their inverses supply a lower bound. The class-number formula gives $h=\sqrt{|\Delta|}L(1,\chi_\Delta)/\pi$. Through one period of length $Q=|\Delta|$ the absolute harmonic sum is at most $1+\log Q$. For the remaining tail, summation by parts and the zero character sum on each full period give an absolute bound of 1. Thus $h\le\lceil\sqrt Q\rceil(2+\operatorname{bitlength}(Q))/3$. This bound is wide. On the 2,048-bit comparison input its initial table length has 518 bits. We stopped after 64 compositions, with the whole product unresolved. Using the earlier conditional Euler-product interval gives fifth-root-scale work up to logarithmic factors, still around $2^{410}$ for the power alone. A substantially cheaper way of obtaining a narrow certified interval or working directly with a suitable class remains open. [Class-number formula, approximation and cost](quadratic-class-number.md) · [Bounded full-size fixture](../benchmarks/class-orders.json)

The implementation composes forms by multiplying their ideal lattices. Put $s=\Delta\bmod2$, $k=(s-\Delta)/4$ and $\omega=(s+\sqrt\Delta)/2$, so $\omega^2=s\omega-k$. A form $[a,b,c]$ corresponds to $(a,\omega-t)$ for $t=(b+s)/2$. If the other leading coefficient and offset are $a',v$, the product has generators with coordinates $(aa',0),(-av,a),(-a't,a'),(tv-k,s-t-v)$ in the basis $(1,\omega)$. Write $d=\gcd(a,a',s-t-v)$. Since $t^2-st+k\equiv0\pmod a$, $d$ also divides $tv-k$. Dividing out the scalar $d$ gives a primitive ideal. Bézout on the second coordinates yields a vector $(z,d)$, and the divided ideal contains $(z/d,1)$. Its index is $aa'/d^2$ by multiplicativity of ideal norm. These determine its canonical two generators and the equivalent reduced form. The repository implementation was written directly from these identities. Lean checks the two-generator product identity and the order arguments. The ideal correspondence, class-number estimate and genus theory are used on paper. ## Thirty-two fresh products

The SHAKE256 domain was fixed as `shared-quadratic-class-orders-v1`. For each index from 0 through 31, two independent streams supplied the first distinct odd 17-bit primes passing trial primality. Only the product went into either class search. Both used primes through 43 and a budget of 100,000 compositions. A third method tried odd divisors from $\lceil\sqrt{N/2}\rceil$ to $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$, stopping at the first factor. Times sum the per-input median of three runs, rotating the method order. All 32 reference factorizations were checked with Lean. [Sampler and certificate verification](../experiments/class_order_holdout.py) · [All 32 inputs and outcomes](../research/class-order-holdout.json)

| Method | Products solved | Windows searched | Operations counted | Total time on this machine |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Independent windows | 31 of 32 | 65 | 86,408 form compositions | 0.137 s |
| Shared windows | 31 of 32 | 41 | 44,221 form compositions | 0.067 s |
| Balanced odd trial division | 32 of 32 | — | 294,118 integer divisibility tests | 0.013 s |

Nineteen products used the same number of compositions in both class searches. Sharing saved work on the other thirteen. On 7,237,264,777, combining involutions gave $71{,}593\times101{,}089$ in 1,702 compositions, down from 10,374. On 8,196,524,729 both searches exhausted their prime menu with no factor. A separate follow-up extending the limit to 59 split it into $66{,}791\times122{,}719$ in 1,949 compositions. The table retains the outcome at the original cutoff.
