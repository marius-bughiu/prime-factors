# What a negative square says about every divisor

Suppose the product $N$ alone yields a number $D\equiv3\pmod4$ and a witness $t$ with $t^2\equiv-D\pmod N$. Check that $\gcd(D,N)=1$. Every odd positive divisor $f$ of $N$ then has Jacobi symbol $(f/D)=+1$. This gives information about each hidden prime separately. Exactly half the invertible residues modulo $D$ have the required sign, even if $D$ has repeated prime factors. Across a complete period for $k$ pairwise coprime such moduli, exactly one in $2^k$ invertible residue classes passes all tests. This says how many classes survive once the witnesses have been found. It gives no bound on the time to find small enough moduli or enumerate the surviving positions. [Lean proofs of the divisor test, square removal and combined count](../PrimeFactors/ReciprocalDivisorClasses.lean)

Indeed $t^2\equiv-D\pmod f$. The symbol $(-D/f)$ equals one by coprimality, and quadratic reciprocity gives $(f/D)=(-D/f)$ since $D\equiv3\pmod4$. Negating the numerator switches the Jacobi sign, so exactly half the units have each sign. The Chinese remainder theorem makes the counts multiply for coprime moduli. [Formal Jacobi reciprocity](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LegendreSymbol/JacobiSymbol.html)

The same reciprocity also constrains the **sum** of the primes. Take any odd $a$ below $2\sqrt{N}$ and put $D=4N-a^2$. For the true sum $S=p+q$, both $(S-a\mid D)$ and $(S+a\mid D)$ must be zero or positive. This follows from $(q-p)^2+D=(S-a)(S+a)$, applied to both positive odd factors on the right. For prime $D$ with $a\not\equiv0\pmod D$, exactly $(D+1)/4$ sum residues pass, half as many as the usual square-discriminant check admits. [Lean proof of the two signs and exact prime count](../PrimeFactors/ReciprocalSumCharacter.lean)

For **135,229**, the largest odd integer below $2\sqrt{N}$ is 735, giving $D=691$. Within the allowable sum interval, ordinary square tests modulo 3, 5, 7 and 691 leave 746, 766 and 770. The extra signs eliminate 746 and 766. At 770, $770^2-4N=228^2$, which gives $(770-228)/2=271$ and $(770+228)/2=499$. Lean checks both primes and the product.
