# Can the modular factors agree at every precision?

They can agree even for a wrong sum. For each of the more than $2^{765}$ wrong sums in the preceding result, there is a **single consistent choice of two factor residues across every power of all 117 primes through 643**. They have the proposed sum and the right product at every depth. Insisting that later answers agree with earlier answers eliminates zero of these wrong candidates. The population and ordering are the same as in the pre-success bound. [Population and ordering](wrong-sums-before-the-search-can-stop.md) · [Lean equivalence](../PrimeFactors/JointLocalMidpointTower.lean)

Let $K$ be the product of the 117 primes. At each depth $e$, the Chinese remainder theorem assembles square roots of $h^2-N$ from the separate primes into a root modulo $K^e$. A deeper root reduces to a shallower one. There are finitely many roots at every level and at least one at every depth. Kőnig’s infinity lemma gives a choice $r_e$ at every depth for which all the reductions agree. The corresponding residues $h-r_e$ and $h+r_e$ always multiply to $N$ and add to $2h$. This also works when tests ask for different depths at different primes. Some individual roots can have no continuation. Only one consistent sequence is required for the result. [Root-sequence proof](../PrimeFactors/CoherentMidpointRoots.lean) · [Mathlib’s finite inverse-system lemma, by Peter Nelson, Apache 2.0](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Order/KonigLemma.html#exists_seq_forall_proj_of_forall_finite)

**The size of an ordinary representative distinguishes the two situations.** Write $D=h^2-N\ge0$ and take a modulus $M>D$. If an integer $x$ represents a modular square root, its square is one of $D,D+M,D+2M,\ldots$. For a nonsquare $D$ this forces $x^2\ge D+M$ for *every* signed representative. If $M>N$ too, $D+M>h^2$, so $|x|>h$. The proposed factors $h-x,h+x$ then cannot both be nonnegative integers. A genuine half-gap $(q-p)/2$ stays the same size at every precision. Applying this height test to a specified candidate still leaves the problem of locating the right candidate. [Gap, recognition threshold and checked examples](../PrimeFactors/ModularRootHeight.lean)

For the wrong midpoint 12,090 of $N=145{,}684{,}811$, the first ten primes have product $M=6{,}469{,}693{,}230>N$. The integer 3,593,537 is a root of $483{,}289$ modulo that entire product. Every representative of every such root has magnitude greater than 12,090. At the true midpoint 12,594, the ordinary half-gap is 3,595. [Original ordered example](wrong-sums-before-the-search-can-stop.md)

The bound can be attained exactly. For $143=11\times13$, the actual midpoint is 12. The proposed midpoint 20 gives $D=257$, a nonsquare congruent to 1 modulo 8. It has compatible roots at every power of two. At $M=2^{14}$, $129^2=257+M$ exactly. This is a separate example about root size. [Dyadic lifting proof](../PrimeFactors/CoherentMidpointRoots.lean)
