# What the Cogentic factoring bound covers

The November 25, 2026 Cogentic preprint describes a randomized algorithm for complete integer factorization. **For a 2,048-bit input the published algorithm selects trial division.** Definition 2.7 sets its cutoff at $2^{10000}$. Step 1 of Definition 6.1 handles every input at or below that cutoff by trial division and certifies the resulting prime factors. This includes every product in our 2,048-bit comparison set. [Paper, page 44](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/ff7ba34ff3028a22bd7ff3b7a89b6fdcc306b690/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf#page=44)

The authors claim a worst-case expected time bound of $L_N[1/3,105]$ and deterministic space bound of $L_N[1/3,40]$. The expectation is over internal random choices for each fixed input. These bounds describe growth as the input size tends to infinity. The large-input argument is of interest for how it proves an adequate supply of relations between integers with small prime factors. The authors report completing a Lean 4.35.0 formalization. Their published directory at the pinned revision contains the paper and README and points to a future companion release for the formalization. [Paper and definitions](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/ff7ba34ff3028a22bd7ff3b7a89b6fdcc306b690/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf#page=2) · [Release directory and CC BY 4.0 paper license](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/ff7ba34ff3028a22bd7ff3b7a89b6fdcc306b690/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization)

## A part we checked independently

The paper counts ways to write a number as one distinguished integer times $s$ primes, all in the same interval $(Y/2,Y]$. Suppose such a representation exists and no prime in the interval divides the number twice. When the distinguished integer is prime, every ordering of the $s+1$ factors works. If it is composite, none of the interval primes can divide it. The composite must stay in the distinguished position and only the other $s$ factors can exchange places. Dividing the number of orderings by $(s+1)!$ gives exactly 1 or $1/(s+1)$. Our Lean proof covers arbitrary $s$. The paper uses $s=4u-1$. [Definition 3.4 and Lemma 3.5(1), Google Research, CC BY 4.0](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/ff7ba34ff3028a22bd7ff3b7a89b6fdcc306b690/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf#page=18) · [Independent proof](../PrimeFactors/HybridSmoothWeight.lean)

For $Y=20$, the factors of $46,189=19\cdot11\cdot13\cdot17$ allow 24 orderings. Replacing 19 with 15 gives $36,465$ and just six orderings. The normalized counts are 1 and $1/4$. The paper's efficient evaluation of this weight takes the factorization of its argument as input. We have checked the counting identity and the way a small common divisor is confined to the distinguished integer. The paper's analytic estimates and overall time bound are separate results.
