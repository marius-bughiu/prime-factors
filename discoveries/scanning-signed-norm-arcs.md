# Finding factors on both ellipses and hyperbolas

A complete scan recovered all eight independently generated products in this comparison. On the four with 20-bit primes, using the smaller unit intervals and exact integer slope calculations reached the first factors in **402 ms** total, against **614 ms** for direct enumeration of the same intervals. Direct enumeration was faster at 16 bits per prime. At 2,048 bits even the smaller intervals require more than $2^{685}$ blocks on both inputs we tested. ## What a complete search guarantees

D. H. and Emma Lehmer published a three-equation factoring method in 1974. They used a table indexed by the product modulo 24 and gave finite intervals for its representations. The signed menu used here agrees with their discriminants in seven of the eight unit classes modulo 24. [Lehmer and Lehmer, *A New Factorization Technique Using Quadratic Forms*, pp. 625–627](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1974-0342458-5)

Let $N=pq$ for distinct primes greater than 3. From the square classes generated by $-1,2,3$, the public Jacobi signs select three signed targets $D$ whose product is a rational square. At least one target is a square at both hidden primes. Each of its four modular roots has an integer representative satisfying

$$x^2-Dy^2=mN,\qquad m\in\{-2,-1,1,2\},\qquad x^2+|D|y^2<3N,$$

with $y$ invertible modulo $N$. Changing signs of the coordinates changes the root $x/y$ at most by an overall minus sign, so enumerating absolute coordinates covers the two inequivalent root pairs. Two roots $r,s$ that differ even after allowing $r=-s$ give a proper factor by $\gcd(r-s,N)$. [Three-target proof](../PrimeFactors/ThreeRootTargets.lean) · [Short representatives and recovery](../PrimeFactors/SmallDiscriminantLattice.lean)

The **broad search** in this comparison includes every nonnegative point under that strict height bound, including solutions with $|m|=2$ when $|D|\le3$. It checks the axes and skips the impossible negative $m$ for negative $D$. Direct and arc scans both reject equations that fail modulo 16 or 3. Negative $D$ uses the existing two ellipse charts, assigning their common boundary to one chart. [Earlier ellipse search](searching-norm-directions.md)

For $D>0$, write $b z^2=M+a u^2$ with $M=|m|N$. For $m>0$, take $u=y,z=x,a=D,b=1$. For $m<0$, take $u=x,z=y,a=1,b=D$. The exact endpoint is $2a u^2\le3N-1-M$. The graph is increasing and convex with curvature at most $a/\sqrt{bM}$. On a block $u=L+j$, approximate its slope by a rational $v/w$. Every integer point then has an integer intercept $wz-vj$ between two computable rational bounds. Each tested intercept gives a quadratic in $j$. The implementation checks its integer roots, divisibility, block endpoints and original equation. For fixed $D,m$, block width of order $N^{1/6}$ keeps the number of intercepts per block bounded. Lean proves at most five intercepts on a hyperbola block at the chosen radius. A full scan uses $O(N^{1/3})$ blocks with polynomial cost in $\log N$ per block. [Hyperbola bounds](../PrimeFactors/HyperbolicNormArc.lean) · [Integer scanner](../experiments/signed_norm_arcs.py)

## Measured costs

The panel contains the first four distinct prime pairs at each width from the SHAKE256 domain `signed-norm-arc-holdout-v1/<bits>`. Every search receives just the product. All eight complete direct and arc scans returned precisely the same 59 representations in total. The following CPU times are the sums of individual product medians over two alternating orders on ARM64 macOS, Python 3.9.6. They include setup, candidate reconstruction and gcds. Lean verification is timed separately from search.

| Bits per prime | Direct square roots at candidate coordinates | Arc blocks | Quadratic lines tested | CPU to first factor, direct / arcs | CPU to finish, direct / arcs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 1,210,660 | 80,209 | 32,238 | 74 / 299 ms | 212 / 765 ms |
| 20 | 18,681,437 | 489,586 | 208,141 | 1.13 / 1.78 s | 3.40 / 4.92 s |

The arcs also take two endpoint square roots per block, plus square roots of nonnegative line discriminants. In the 20-bit panel that was 979,172 endpoint roots and 206,597 discriminant roots. The original implementation used Python's `Fraction` type for each ellipse enclosure. A separate oracle scanning $x$ directly checked all 4,064 signed equations for $2\le N\le128$, including prime, square and repeated-factor inputs, zero coordinates, and strict endpoint exclusions. [Per-product work and timings](../research/signed-norm-arcs.json) · [Automated checks](../tests/test_signed_norm_arcs.py)

The fresh 2,048-bit input started on a hyperbola. Its first 64 blocks covered $1.727\times10^{-205}$ of all chart positions across the public menu. The crossed input started on an ellipse and covered $1.315\times10^{-205}$. Both prefixes ruled out every intercept in their blocks without finding a representation. Complete scans of these menus require about $2^{685.90}$ and $2^{686.55}$ blocks respectively. The separately capped direct searches tried 4,096 positions, covering $5.148\times10^{-306}$ and $3.282\times10^{-306}$. Neither recovered a factor. [Exact denominators and work caps](../research/signed-norm-arcs.json)

## Shorter intervals and integer-only slope calculations

The unit-reduced search needs only one multiplier per target. For $D=-1,-2,-3,2$, use $m=1$. For $D=-6$, use $m=1$ if $N\equiv1\pmod3$, and $m=2$ otherwise. For $D=3,6$, choose $m=1$ or $-1$ according as $N\equiv1$ or $2\pmod3$. When $D>0$, multiplication by a norm-one unit supplies a representative satisfying $2Dy^2\le(U-m)N$, with $U=3,2,5$ for $D=2,3,6$. These intervals preserve every modular root while allowing its integer representative to change. We compared the complete set of roots up to an overall sign across the two domains. Within each domain we compared every integer point and every arc operation count. [Unit interval](../PrimeFactors/NormUnitInterval.lean) · [Multiplier choices and completeness](../PrimeFactors/ThreeNormCurves.lean)

The follow-up also calculated both ellipse slope bounds as integer numerator–denominator pairs and rounded only the final intercept endpoints. This gave exactly the same lines as the original reduced-fraction implementation. In the same eight products, changing the intervals reduced the 16-bit coordinate count from 1,456,140 to 660,865, and the 20-bit count from 22,766,140 to 9,483,511.

| Bits per prime | Search interval | Direct, first / complete | Integer-only arcs, first / complete |

| 16 | Broad | 76.9 / 215.9 ms | 104.3 / 298.9 ms |
| 16 | Unit-reduced | 46.6 / 98.5 ms | 65.7 / 143.6 ms |

An independent enumeration modulo each of 190 products of distinct primes $5\le p<q\le79$ recovered exactly the same 460 target root classes as the two domains. On the two full-size inputs, exact chart counts put the unit-reduced search at $2^{685.18}$ and $2^{685.23}$ blocks. The 64-block prefixes covered respectively $3.079\times10^{-205}$ and $3.653\times10^{-205}$ of the eligible coordinate positions and returned no factor.
