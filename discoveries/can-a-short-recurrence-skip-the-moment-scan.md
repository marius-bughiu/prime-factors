# Can a short recurrence skip the Jacobi moment scan?

For a product of two distinct odd primes, the first Jacobi moment that departs from a known formula reveals a factor. Could we jump to it by learning a fixed rule from the early values? Lean proves that a rule expressing each moment as the same linear combination of the previous ones must reach back at least $q-3$ places if it works throughout the positive degrees, where $q$ is the larger prime. Keeping only even degrees still requires at least $(q-3)/2$ places. The coefficients may depend on the product. [Moment formula and first exceptional degree](when-jacobi-moments-first-change.md) · [Recurrence proof](../PrimeFactors/JacobiMomentRecurrence.lean)

For example, take $77=7\cdot11$. Its first departure occurs at degree six, where the measured moment is 42 and the formula predicts 20. Reducing the positive-degree sequence modulo 11 gives a sequence of exact minimum order eight. Keeping only degrees $2,4,6,\ldots$ gives exact minimum order four modulo 11. Any fixed rule modulo 77 for the corresponding entire sequence must satisfy those lower bounds, since reducing its coefficients modulo 11 preserves its predictions. [Checked example](../PrimeFactors/ProjectiveWordMoments.lean)

## Why do fixed rules need so many terms?

Write $N=pq$, with $p<q$. The degree-$k$ moment sums $t^k$ times the Jacobi symbol of $t^2-4$ over every residue modulo $N$. Reduced modulo $q$, its positive-degree values are a weighted sum of powers of exactly $q-3$ distinct nonzero numbers. The bases $t=\pm2$ have zero weight, and $t=0$ contributes nothing in a positive degree. A linear recurrence has a characteristic polynomial. If the recurrence holds for $q-3$ consecutive starting positions, the Vandermonde determinant on the distinct bases forces this polynomial to vanish at each of them. Its degree is therefore at least $q-3$. Taking the product of the linear factors for those bases attains the bound modulo $q$. For even degrees, $t$ and $-t$ share a square and their equal nonzero weights add, halving the number of distinct bases. [Proofs of the bounds and matching local recurrences](../PrimeFactors/JacobiMomentRecurrence.lean)

## What if we stop before the first departure?

Below degree $p-1$ the even moments equal the middle binomial coefficients, $M_{2j}=\binom{2j}{j}\bmod N$. A separate finite-prefix theorem applies here. If one normalized constant-coefficient rule of order $s$ matches all the even moments from index $s+1$ through $m$, with $2m<p-1$, then $m<2(s+1)$. Matching everything through degree $p-3$ already forces $s\ge\lfloor(p-3)/4\rfloor$. The proof uses the coefficient of $X^j$ in $((1+X)^2)^j$ and the existing theorem on finite prefixes of polynomial coefficient sequences. [Finite-prefix theorem](../PrimeFactors/HasseRecurrenceDegree.lean) · [Application to Jacobi moments](../PrimeFactors/JacobiMomentRecurrence.lean)

Order measures how far back a rule reaches. A long rule can still be sparse. For example, modulo $q$ the positive moments repeat after $q-1$ degrees by Fermat's little theorem, although this period uses the unknown prime. A recurrence whose coefficients vary with the degree, or a direct formula for a single chosen degree, can have a different cost. For 1,024-bit prime factors the whole even sequence requires fixed order at least $2^{1022}-2$; finding an informative degree from the product and evaluating it cheaply remains open.
