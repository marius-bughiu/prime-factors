# Lifting the midpoint and gap together

Write an odd product as $N=pq$, and put $h=(p+q)/2$ and $g=(q-p)/2$ with $p\le q$. A search that guesses both coordinates can discard joint guesses that pass each coordinate’s separate test. For $N=77$ and modulus 64, the separate tests admit eight possible midpoints and sixteen possible gaps. Of the 128 combinations, exactly 32 satisfy the coupled test. The actual pair is $h=9$, $g=2$, giving $7\cdot11=77$. [Lean proof and checked counts](../PrimeFactors/DyadicSumGap.lean)

For a power of two $M\ge4$, the coupled test on a pair of residues is $h^2-g^2\equiv N\pmod{2M}$. Changing a representative by $M$ preserves that test. Exactly $M/2$ pairs pass. Every such pair has exactly two children among its four possible lifts modulo $2M$. Repeated lifts are always possible. Looking at all higher powers of two therefore cannot eliminate a surviving prefix if the search has no other information. Projecting onto the midpoint alone gives exactly the usual test that $h^2-N$ is a square modulo $2M$. A search over midpoints alone gains no extra exclusion from retaining the gap coordinate.
