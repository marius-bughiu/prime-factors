# What a subexponential tagged contraction costs

A polynomial-valued reading of the Rudich–Rousseau count gives a uniformly constructible arithmetic circuit over the field of three elements. A separate quantitative tensor theorem computes the divisor sum in one contraction using polynomial space. The latter also applies to inputs with more than two prime factors. These are paper deductions. The [checked local circuit results](counting-the-factor-sum.md) and the original factoring theorems have separate scopes.

## Extracting a circuit from the pinned counting engine

Let $n$ be the binary length of $N\ge2$, and $z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1}$ indeterminates. The polynomial to construct is
$$
P_N(z)=\sum_{d\mid N}\prod_{i:\,d_i=1}z_i.
$$
In the first input gate for block $r$, digit $j$ and bit $h$ correspond to position $i=L(r+Aj)+h$. Multiply its original predicate by $z_i$ when that bit is one and $i<n$. Each bit belongs to exactly one block. The original unique-filling argument then gives precisely the indicated monomial for each divisor. For compatibility with the upstream prefix interface $k<n$, add the two counts for $k=n-1$ and high bit 0 or 1. The two networks have the same public dimensions. **Paper proposition.** The counting schedule at revision `c91cc3f` yields a uniform, division-free circuit of length $2^{o(n)}$ for $2^E P_N$ over $\mathbb F_3$, with known $E$. Its construction takes $2^{o(n)}$ bit operations. The following details are needed to extract a circuit from this particular algorithm.

1. At a level of side $G$ and width $\lambda$, set $t=\lceil\lambda/3\rceil$ and $r=\binom G2^2$. Each genuine choice assigns one of $t+1$ pieces to each of the $r$ rectangles. Both the row and column contraction for that **same choice** equal $Z_\iota$, and the sum over genuine choices is $2^{tr}$ times the parent value. These identities hold over every commutative ring, hence over the formal polynomial ring. The compiled count encodes choices on all $G^4$ quadruples and therefore repeats each genuine choice $(t+1)^{G^4-r}$ times. Keep a code exactly when its digits off the rectangles are zero. One can traverse the original schedule, setting each noncanonical path's contribution to zero. Over the chain the surviving multiplier is $2^E$, where $E=\sum_j t_jr_j$; the public width recurrence makes $E$ common to all paths. Its inverse in $\mathbb F_3$ is $(-1)^E$. This removes the need for a prime search. [Ring-valued choice identities](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/Level.lean) · [Multiplicity of padding](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/EngineGrid.lean#L90-L137) · [Chain and widths](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/EngineGroup.lean#L96-L186)
2. Use two data types in the source interpreter. Settings, shape, loop counters, signs of fixed decomposition coefficients, grouping codes, equality tests and addresses use ordinary integers. Tensor weights use references to arithmetic gates. In coarsening, the node weight multiplies old weights and contracts internal edge factors. Its setting counts and widths read only the grouping and old sizes or widths. At a grid level, the previous table is read in the line weight. The row/column comparison and first-good-group search use widths. The clique and brute-force sums bound their loops by setting counts and dimensions. Table lookup uses the node, boundary code and slot. Thus no control decision needs a residue of a tagged weight. Emit a gate for each weight addition or multiplication, use the field constant $-1$ for signs, and map table writes to fresh references. This retains sharing when old table slots get overwritten. Compiled integer arithmetic does contain value-dependent loops for modulus and multiplication, so the markings belong on the source expressions **before** that compilation. [Coarsening](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/EngineCoarsenEx.lean#L42-L107) · [Grid weight and width](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/EngineGridEx.lean#L379-L400) · [Level choice](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/EngineLevelEx.lean#L48-L68) · [Tables and paths](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/UniEngine.lean#L67-L86)
3. For finite accounting let $T$ be the sum of the two unmodified one-modulus command costs at modulus 3 after setup. Each marked arithmetic site and each fold iteration executes at least one command on this untagged run. The compiler evaluates both operands of arithmetic and visits every iteration of a bounded product, including after a zero factor. Integer comparisons on structural data take the same branches. Each input-weight visit incurs at most $n$ additional tag multiplications, and the canonical-code test scans $G^4$ public digits per level of a path. For positive depth, the selected top clique size satisfies $K_{\rm top}^{40}\le n$ and $G^2\le K_{\rm top}$ at every transformed level, so $G^4\le n$. There are no rectangle tests at depth zero. Consequently the number of emitted gates is $O((n+1)(T+1))$. The command bound for each count is $60Y(V)^3$, where $W=4V^2$, $Y(V)=(W+1)[64(W+1)^3]^{100}$ and the upstream construction supplies $V=2^{o(n)}$. Setup has the same subexponential bound. Integer masks, tag indices, and gate addresses cost polynomial overhead in $n+T+V$ even with a sequential tape scan for every reference. For a circuit with $H$ gates, saving operands, values, and adjoints takes $O((H+n)\log(H+n+2))$ bits. This argument does not supply polynomial space for reverse differentiation. A kernel-checked refinement would need the two data types, canonical-code filtering and their cost comparison formalized. The padded schedule also works without changing its choice enumeration. Let $H$ be the maximum of 2 and all the integers $t_j+1$. A prime $H<\ell<2H$ exists by Bertrand's postulate. All prime divisors of the padded multiplier divide $2\prod_j(t_j+1)$, so its residue at $\ell$ is invertible. The upstream bound $M=n+4+\sum_j2G_j^4t_j$ exceeds $H$ and its budget covers every modulus up to $2M$. Enumerating and trial-testing the integers in $(H,2H)$ takes $\operatorname{poly}(M)$ bit operations, still $2^{o(n)}$. Thus one selected modulus suffices for a field-valued readout even with the original multiplicities. ## One contraction for the exact divisor sum

**Paper corollary.** Given any positive $n$-bit integer $N$, one weighted Boolean tensor contraction computes both $\tau(N)$ and $\sigma(N)$ deterministically in $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ bit operations and $n^{O(1)}$ space, asymptotically. Shunia's Theorem 1 already gives this factoring time bound using prefix queries. The following deduction from its underlying theorems changes the observable and the number of contraction calls. [Shunia, Theorem 1 and Lemma 4](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00711v1)

Start with the parsimonious multiplier of Akshima et al., Theorem 3, on two $n$-bit inputs. It has fewer than $1100(n+1)$ edges, degree below $136(n+1)^{3/4}$, polynomial description length and polynomial-time local predicates. Subdivide each edge with equality to obtain a bipartite network. Pin the $2n$ output bits to $N$. At the equality vertex for input bit $i$ of the first operand, give the equal-one case weight $1+2^i V$ and the equal-zero case weight $1$. Use the ring $(\mathbb Z/2^P)[V]/(V^2)$ with $P=n+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$. The degree and description bounds survive, and $P=O(n)$. Theorem D.1 therefore applies with $s=\Theta(n)$, chosen large enough for its degree bound. [Akshima et al., original multiplier paper](https://github.com/bochum-crypto/subexponential-ecdl/blob/main/subexponential-ecdl.pdf) · [OpenAI, quantitative Boolean tensor contraction, Theorem D.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-reduction.tex)

For each divisor $d$, its unique filling contributes $1+dV$ because $V^2=0$. The two coefficients of the contraction are consequently $\tau(N)$ and $\sigma(N)$ modulo $2^P$. The divisor involution $d\mapsto N/d$ gives $\sigma(N)\le N\sum_{j=1}^N 1/j\le nN<2^P$ by grouping the reciprocals in dyadic blocks. Since $\tau(N)\le\sigma(N)$, both canonical residues are exact. For $N=pq$ with distinct primes, subtract $1+N$ from the linear coefficient to obtain $p+q$. At $N=143$ this reads $4+168V$, giving the sum $24$. The bound controls asymptotic complexity. An implementation and measured contraction cost at 2048 bits remain open.

All bit marginals can also be retained simultaneously. In $(\mathbb Z/4)[V]/(V^{2n})$, tag a set bit $i$ by $1+V^{n+i}$. Every cross term vanishes. Coefficient $n+i$ counts divisors with that bit set modulo four. For a product of distinct primes, subtract the known contributions of $1$ and $N$ before taking the canonical residue. The result is the ordinary bit count $0,1$, or $2$ for the two primes. This instance meets Theorem D.1 with $P_0=2$, $\ell=2n$ and uses its polynomial-space algorithm directly.

Lean also checks a single scalar-ring version for **every positive input**. Set $T=2^{2n}$ and use $1+T2^i$ on each set bit of the first input. In $\mathbb Z/2^{4n}\mathbb Z$ the exact representative is $\tau(N)+T\sigma(N)$. The bounds $\tau(N)\le N$ and $\sigma(N)\le N^2<T$ suffice. For $N=12$, kernel enumeration of all 256 four-bit input assignments gives $7174=6+256\cdot28$. Bach, Miller and Shallit's Theorem 2 then gives randomized polynomial-time recovery of the complete factorization from $N$ and the high digit $\sigma(N)$. The related totient count gives a deterministic decoder under the Dirichlet hypothesis.
