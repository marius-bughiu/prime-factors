What a product reveals about its factors

We can factor six specially constructed 2,048-bit examples. Finding the two primes of an ordinary 2,048-bit product efficiently remains open. We study which guesses the product lets us rule out.

The factor sum

S² − 4N = (q − p)²

Knowing S = p + q determines both primes. The product restricts S differently modulo each small prime.

What the proofs establish

Can we recognize the right guess?

For two distinct 1,024-bit primes with a 2,048-bit product, 1,040 random checks can identify their average with over 99.9999% probability if we test every candidate. On a 41-bit example, we tested all 41,807 candidates to find the one that passed.

One remainder gives a prime

A binomial coefficient computed from the product gives the larger prime exactly. On a 31-bit product the direct calculation produced a 761,262-bit intermediate. Computing the remainder efficiently at 2,048 bits is the open step.

One L-value determines the sum

A single boundary reading at height 1 determines the sum of two balanced primes to the nearest integer. Obtaining the reading efficiently remains open.
