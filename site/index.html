What a product reveals about its factors

If you multiply two randomly chosen 1,024-bit primes and give us only the result, we have no practical way to find them in general. We have recovered the primes in specially constructed 2,048-bit examples. For one constructed example, we chose a clue after looking at the published factors. The same clue missed both primes on a freshly generated product. How we chose the clue.

The factor sum

S² − 4N = (q − p)²

Knowing S = p + q determines both primes. The product restricts S differently modulo each small prime.
