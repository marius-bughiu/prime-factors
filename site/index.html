Computational number theory

What a product reveals about its factors

Given a 2,048-bit integer N = pq, what can we deduce about its two unknown primes? We study which possibilities the product excludes and what work remains to recover the factors.

The factor sum

S² − 4N = (q − p)²

Knowing S = p + q determines both primes. The product restricts S differently modulo each small prime.

What the proofs establish

One prime at a time

Modulo an odd prime coprime to N, every nonzero residue can represent one factor. Only about half the residues can represent the sum. The exact count depends on whether N is a square modulo that prime.

Read the counting theorem

Combining constraints

For coprime moduli, the numbers of allowed sum classes multiply. For N = 143, just six classes survive modulo 35. The theorem counts a full period; a short interval can contain several survivors.

Read the CRT proof

Isolating the true sum

For two distinct 1,024-bit primes, a deduction from Dirichlet GRH shows that tests at odd primes below three million isolate the sum in its balanced interval. An efficient way to retrieve it remains open.
