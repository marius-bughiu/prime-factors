What a product reveals about its factors

We can factor six specially constructed 2,048-bit examples. Finding the two primes of an ordinary 2,048-bit product efficiently remains open. We study which guesses the product lets us rule out.

The factor sum

S² − 4N = (q − p)²

Knowing S = p + q determines both primes. The product restricts S differently modulo each small prime.

What the proofs establish

A search of the full balanced range for 1,900,014,700,027 took 1,708 strips when we grouped factors by their remainders modulo 6, down from 5,111. It recovered the primes 1,000,003 and 1,900,009. Can we recognize the right guess?

For two distinct 1,024-bit primes with a 2,048-bit product, 1,040 random checks can identify their average with over 99.9999% probability if we test every candidate. On a 41-bit example, we tested all 41,807 candidates to find the one that passed.

One remainder gives a prime

A binomial coefficient computed from the product gives the larger prime exactly. On a 31-bit product the direct calculation produced a 761,262-bit intermediate. Computing the remainder efficiently at 2,048 bits is the open step.

Can one number reveal both primes?

On 32 full-size products, one value rounded to 1,366 fractional bits let us recover both primes with one square test. We used known primes to generate each value. We also computed a shorter value directly from 60,491 and recovered 241 and 251. Finding a cheap way to compute it at full size remains open.

Which continued fractions yield factors?

Combining 53 intermediate results revealed the primes of a 79-bit product. On a 2,048-bit test, every combination of the first 65,536 results from one walk failed the square test.

How much precision do separate readings need?

An alternative uses 89 sums rounded to 53 fractional bits each. A higher-precision sum can supply several readings. For 667 we used one sum of 616 terms to recover 23 and 29. Its setup also encounters the factors.

Fewer guesses in the worst case

For two similarly sized primes, we derive a way to rule out more guesses. The full bound awaits independent review. The search recovered both primes of a 79-bit product from random prime draws. At 2,048 bits the main sieve requires at least 2400 residue visits or table entries.
