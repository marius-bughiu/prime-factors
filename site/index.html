What a product reveals about its factors

Given a 2,048-bit integer N = pq, what can we deduce about its two unknown primes? We study which possibilities the product excludes and what work remains to recover the factors.

The factor sum

S² − 4N = (q − p)²

Knowing S = p + q determines both primes. The product restricts S differently modulo each small prime.

What the proofs establish

Two square roots on a 2048-bit input

When (2p + 1)(2q + 1) is a square and the primes are balanced, two integer square roots determine both factors. The benchmark receives only the product.

Read every bit from one circuit

A tagged multiplication circuit gives the factor sum in one forward and one reverse pass. At ten bits per factor, building the circuit took 157,515 row joins. The reverse pass used seven arithmetic gates.

One L-value determines the sum

A single boundary reading at height 1 determines the sum of two balanced primes to the nearest integer. Obtaining the reading efficiently remains open.
