What a product reveals about its factors

Given a 2,048-bit integer N = pq, what can we deduce about its two unknown primes? We study which possibilities the product excludes and what work remains to recover the factors.

The factor sum

S² − 4N = (q − p)²

Knowing S = p + q determines both primes. The product restricts S differently modulo each small prime.

Recover the sum by matching powers

For every unit modulo N, raising it to p + q gives the same value as raising it to N + 1. Two short lists of powers can test many possible sums at once.

When a prime power helps

For 34,387 the tests modulo 3, 5 and 7 leave three possible even sums. Replacing 3 with 9 leaves only 388, giving the factors 137 and 251.
